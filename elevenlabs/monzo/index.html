01:31 · why this can be accepted without asking anyone

Why Monzo can say yes to the first step without a procurement

This is the question that decides whether anything else on the page matters, so it gets its own answer rather than a line in a list.

A regulated firm cannot let a third party’s software talk to its customers because someone filled in a web form. That is a conduct decision with rules attached, and rightly so. It cannot hand customer data to an unappointed vendor, and it cannot let a vendor into its telephony on a demo.

None of those things is being asked. The first exchange is a phone number, travelling in one direction, built from guidance Monzo published itself. Ringing a number is not a procurement, it creates no obligation, it needs no contract, and it needs no supplier record. It is the same act as listening to a voicemail somebody left you.

The practical test is a good one: what would this look like written down? Written down, it is: an outside party built a demonstration from our own public help pages and gave us a number to ring. We were not asked for anything and we gave nothing. That sentence survives a risk committee reading it, which is the only bar that counts.

Everything past the first step changes shape, and the page says so rather than letting it blur. A live deployment is a commercial arrangement with ElevenLabs, it goes through your procurement and your assurance people, and it starts with them rather than with anybody on this page.
