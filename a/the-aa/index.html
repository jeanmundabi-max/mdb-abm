Most of that receivable is insurance broking. Why are you showing it to us?

Because your own note says £78m of the £187m is broking, which means the other £109m is something else, and the sixty day plus bucket is not attributed either way. We are not claiming the £36m is member collections. We are pointing out that the fastest growing part of your book cannot currently be split by payment method, and that is worth knowing before the next reporting date.

Our members do not complain about payments.

Correct, and we checked. 500 of your most recent App Store reviews, 334 of them five star, and payment barely features. The loudest complaints are about patrols not arriving, which is not our subject and we are not going to pretend it is. The evidence for this page is your accounts, not your reviews.

We already collect by Direct Debit.

Then the question is not the payment method, it is day two. When a collection fails, what date does the retry land on. The Norwegian Refugee Council called their old approach basically chucking attempts at the wall and reported a 7% improvement in recapture after changing only that.

Is a rising receivable not just growth?

Partly. Your book grew 17.6% and that is growth. The sixty day plus tail grew 44%. Those two numbers moving apart is the thing worth explaining, and it is visible only because you publish the ageing at all, which most companies do not.

What does GoCardless actually claim?

That Success+ recovers up to 70% of failed payments. Up to is their word and we have kept it. Separately they report that 70% of businesses said Success+ improves customer relationships. Two different figures that happen to share a number.

Who has done this in our industry?

Nobody in roadside or motoring is published by GoCardless, so we are not going to invent a peer. The closest published case is Lifestyle Fitness, a membership subscription business with the same monthly collection shape and the same kind of payer.
