Verified Mandates

Objections

The questions worth asking

Our collection is regulated and controlled. Why is this on our desk?

Because your own SFCR lists it as a credit risk you mitigate with document controls and checks. That is an accurate description of a process that can fail, and 875 reviews describe the failures. Nothing here disputes the control. It asks whether there is less to control.

The inactivity fee is our pricing decision.

It is, and it stays yours. Nothing on this page suggests removing it. The observation is narrower: the moment you collect and the moment the rider has least ability to pay are the same moment, and a rail that gives advance notice changes the second one without touching the first.

That £33.6m debtor is intra-group and not overdue.

Correct, your SFCR says so and this page says so twice. It is not evidence of anything and it is not being used as evidence. The collection question here is about riders.

Riders top up because pay as you go is prepaid. That is the product.

Understood. The question is whether the top up has to be a thing the rider remembers. A mandate can fund a balance on a schedule instead, which is the same product with one fewer failure mode.

We already retry failed instalments.

Then the question is which day. The Norwegian Refugee Council described their old approach as basically chucking attempts at the wall and reported a 7% improvement in recapture after changing only the timing.

What does GoCardless actually claim?

That Success+ recovers up to 70% of failed payments. Up to is their word and we have kept it. Separately they report that 70% of businesses said Success+ improves customer relationships. Two different figures that happen to share a number.
